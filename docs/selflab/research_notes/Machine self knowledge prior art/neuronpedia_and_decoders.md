## Q1. Neuronpedia (as of Sept 2026): features, model/SAE coverage (Qwen?), APIs, circuit tracing, self-hosting, auth and rate limits

### Takeaway
Neuronpedia is now an Apache-2.0 monorepo of seven services (webapp/API, Postgres+pgvector, inference, autointerp, graph/circuit-tracer, sparsity, NLA) running on its own `interp-engine`; it hosts SAE/transcoder dashboards, steering (incl. projection-capping), attribution graphs, Jacobian-lens and NLA endpoints with per-IP hourly rate limits. For **Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (NeuralScope's model)** the only hosted tool I could verify is a community NLA at layer 18 (no SAEs, no pre-fitted J-lens, no graphs); Qwen3 (1.7B/4B/8B/14B/32B) and Qwen3.5/3.6 are much better covered.

It is, however, a ready "external-observer arm" (SAE/J-lens/NLA readouts, steering/ablation) against which a model's self-reports could be scored.

## Q2. "Jacobian Lens": origin, computation, difference from logit/tuned lens, implementation and hosting

### Takeaway
The Jacobian lens (J-lens) is from Anthropic's "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models" (Gurnee, Sofroniew, … Lindsey; Transformer Circuits, 2026-07-06). It replaces the logit lens's implicit identity map with a corpus-averaged Jacobian from layer ℓ to the final layer (logit lens = special case J=I). Reference code is `anthropics/jacobian-lens` (Apache-2.0); pre-fitted lenses for ~40 open models are on HF `neuronpedia/jacobian-lens`; interactive readouts/steering at neuronpedia.org/jlens. Its causal "verbal report" experiments are the closest existing evidence that a model's *own* self-reports depend on specific internal content — but they were run on Claude models.

### Cited Findings
- Authors: Wes Gurnee*, Nicholas Sofroniew*, Adam Pearce, Mateusz Piotrowski, Isaac Kauvar, Runjin Chen, Anna Soligo, Paul Bogdan, Euan Ong, Rowan Wang, T. Ben Thompson, David Abrahams, Subhash Kantamneni, Emmanuel Ameisen, Joshua Batson, Jack Lindsey*† (Anthropic), published 2026-07-06 — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper). An arXiv PDF with the same title is listed at [arXiv:2607.15495](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15495) (not opened).
- Definition: "J_ℓ = E_{t, t′≥t, prompt}[∂h_final,t′/∂h_ℓ,t]" over source positions, all subsequent positions and "a corpus of one thousand prompts sampled from a pretraining-like distribution"; readout "lens(h_ℓ) = softmax(W_U norm(J_ℓ h_ℓ))" — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper); code docs: "1000 sequences of 128 tokens… Quality saturates quickly (§9.3); ~100 prompts is usable"; reference implementation "not maintained and not accepting contributions" — [jacobian-lens README](https://github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens) (code-verified).
- Versus logit lens (J=I) and tuned lens (trained affine map to match outputs, which "tends to 'skip ahead' to the output"): J-lens recovers intermediate content where the logit lens reads noise — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper); [nnsight tutorial](https://nnsight.net/tutorials/mini-papers/jacobian-lens/) (secondary tutorial; explains fitting as ceil(d_model/dim_batch) vector-Jacobian backward passes per prompt).
- J-space = sparse non-negative combinations (≲25 active) of J-lens vectors (rows of W_U J_ℓ); claimed five workspace properties: verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalization, selectivity — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper).
- Models: primary Claude Sonnet 4.5, corroboration on Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5, Opus 4.6; no open models in the main paper — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper, per fetched summary).
- Causal validation: concept swaps (e.g., soccer→rugby) change the model's own report of what it thought about, with swap targets reaching top-5 in 88% of trials; two-hop intermediate swaps put the target answer at top-1 in 54% (Haiku) / 70% (Sonnet/Opus); ablating top-10 J-lens directions (layers L38–54) drives multi-hop reasoning near zero but leaves MMLU/SQuAD/sentiment near baseline; injected J-lens vectors are reported in "the majority of trials" in an introspection protocol — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper).
- Limitations: single-token vocabulary concepts only; "an imperfect tool… only approximately and incompletely captures the model's underlying workspace" — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) (paper).
- Hosting: Neuronpedia J-lens launched 2026-07-06 (blog 2026-07-10: "Available for 12 models", 36 pre-fitted lenses at the time, contributions invited); lens API supports steering/ablation of J-lens token vectors — [jacobian-lens post](https://www.neuronpedia.org/blog/jacobian-lens) (code-verified in repo), [lens route](https://github.com/hijohnnylin/neuronpedia/blob/main/apps/webapp/app/api/lens/prompt/route.ts) (code-verified); 40 lens directories on HF as of 2026-09-30, no qwen2.5-1.5b-it — [HF](https://huggingface.co/neuronpedia/jacobian-lens) (code-verified).
- Follow-ups: a theory paper frames J-lens as a first-order causal transfer operator whose Jacobian "energy" is sparse and proposes filtering to top 10–20% energy positions — [arXiv:2608.25347](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25347) (paper, via abstract/highlights). The activation-controllability benchmark shows instructed concept *suppression* evades J-lens readouts (<10% top-10 report rate in 11/13 models) — [arXiv:2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664) (paper).

### Inferences
- **Redundant in NeuralScope:** NeuralScope's Logit Lens should be re-cast as the J=I baseline column of a J-lens view; a Qwen2.5-1.5B lens would have to be fitted locally with `jlens.fit` (d_model 1536, 28 layers, ~100–1,000 prompts) — feasible in principle on CPU but likely hours (untested; inference).
- **Relevance to self-knowledge:** the swap→verbal-report experiment is a template for SelfLab's causal verification: change one internal component, check whether the model's *own* report changes accordingly. That is subject-level evidence (not a separate reader), but it measures the dependence of reports on content, not calibration, and was shown only on Claude models (inference).
- **Missing:** no calibration of J-lens-grounded self-reports; no external-observer baseline (e.g., a second model predicting the report from the transcript); no open-model replication of the verbal-report/introspection protocol in the paper itself (inference).

- I did not read the "multi-token extension" appendix or verify open-model replications ("external replication" on Qwen3.6-27B is mentioned only by a secondary blog). Titles "Interpreting Language Model Hidden States at Scale" (arXiv:2608.10260) and "The First Token Is a Clue" (arXiv:2608.31084) appeared in search but were not read.

## Q3. Natural Language Autoencoders (NLAs)

### Takeaway
NLAs (Fraser-Taliente, Kantamneni, Ong et al., Anthropic, Transformer Circuits, 2026-05-07) pair an Activation Verbalizer (activation→text) with an Activation Reconstructor (text→activation), both initialized as copies of the frozen target and trained with SFT then GRPO to maximize reconstruction (FVE 0.6–0.8). Checkpoints exist for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-3-12B/27B-IT, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, and a community **Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct layer-18** NLA hosted on Neuronpedia. Independent audits show the reconstruction score does not validate individual claims (≈2% of specific claims grounded; >75% assertion risk in released NLAs).

### Cited Findings
- Authors (20): Kit Fraser-Taliente, Subhash Kantamneni, Euan Ong, Dan Mossing, Christina Lu, Paul C. Bogdan, Emmanuel Ameisen, James Chen, Dzmitry Kishylau, Adam Pearce, Julius Tarng, Alex Wu, Jeff Wu, Yang Zhang, Daniel M. Ziegler, Evan Hubinger, Joshua Batson, Jack Lindsey, Samuel Zimmerman, Samuel Marks; 2026-05-07 — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/index.html), [Anthropic post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/natural-language-autoencoders) (paper).
- Mechanism (paper): AV is a copy of target M; the unit-normalized activation is "scaled by a fixed constant factor" and inserted "in place of the special token's embedding" in a fixed prompt; AR = LLM "truncated to its first l layers" + learned affine map at the final token; warm start by SFT on Claude Opus 4.5 summaries (FVE ≈0.3–0.4); AV trained with GRPO, reward −log‖h−AR(z)‖², KL penalty to init; AR trained by MSE; FVE 0.6–0.8 — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/index.html) (paper).
- Mechanism (code): "inject the vector as a single token embedding into a fixed prompt"; activations taken ~2/3 depth; per-checkpoint `nla_meta.yaml`: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct L20/28, d=3584, injection token `㈎` (id 149705), `injection_scale` 150; Gemma-3-12B-IT L32 (scale 80000, plus √d embed scale); Gemma-3-27B-IT L41 (60000); Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct L53 (30); injection is embedding replacement with no learned adapter; released Qwen `fve_nrm` 0.752; training reference "SFT on 2×H100; RL to ~75% FVE on 2×8×H100"; Apache-2.0 code, checkpoints inherit base licences — [README](https://github.com/kitft/natural_language_autoencoders), [docs/inference.md](https://github.com/kitft/natural_language_autoencoders/blob/main/docs/inference.md) (code-verified).
- Claude results: NLA-equipped auditors found hidden misaligned motivations "between 12% and 15% of the time" vs "less than 3%" without NLAs; unverbalized evaluation awareness in 16% (destructive-coding test) and 26% (SWE-bench Verified) vs <1% of real claude.ai transcripts; causal check by steering with NLA-reconstructed vectors (rhyme planning example) — [Anthropic post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/natural-language-autoencoders), [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/index.html) (paper).
- Stated limitations: confabulation ("false claims about the context"), "NLAs are blackboxes by construction", AV "has the capacity to make additional inferences beyond what is stored in an activation", cost (joint RL on two LMs; hundreds of tokens per activation), degenerate solutions (verbatim context or uninterpretable text) — [paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/index.html) (paper).
- **Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct NLA:** AV `dormantx/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-NLA-L18-av` (Apache-2.0 card) reads `hidden_states[18]` ("output of block 18, before the final norm"), d_model 1536, injection token `<|image_pad|>` (id 151655), `injection_scale` 1.01256; the sidecar records `stage: sl`, `loss_type: sft_next_token` — [model card](https://huggingface.co/dormantx/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-NLA-L18-av), [nla_meta.yaml](https://huggingface.co/dormantx/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-NLA-L18-av/blob/main/nla_meta.yaml) (code-verified). Neuronpedia: "surprisingly workable, especially for simple concepts, though we couldn't replicate more complex things like poetry planning" — [jacobian-lens post](https://www.neuronpedia.org/blog/jacobian-lens) (code-verified).
- Other community NLAs: Gemma 4 E2B NLAs "trained on a 4GB consumer GPU" (Caleb DeLeeuw) — [headvis post](https://github.com/hijohnnylin/neuronpedia/blob/main/apps/webapp/app/blog/posts/headvis.mdx) (code-verified); Qwen3-8B NLA by Marco Bazzani (`marco-bazzani/Qwen3-8B-nla`) used in CHIVE — [arXiv:2608.16747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16747) (paper).
- Audit 1 ("Train the Model, Not the Reader", Dingeto, 2026-07-22, v2 2026-08-19): on the released Qwen2.5-7B L20 NLA, explanations reconstruct at r̃=0.84 but flip-tests find grounding in only 4.2%/2.1%/1.6% of salient specific claims (τ=0.02/0.05/0.10); co-trained AV/AR pairs develop "private codes"; a score-maximizing adversary "suppresses about 87% of its lie penalty"; proposed RECAP (co-trained linear heads on the target) lets a probe flag lies at AUC 0.95 vs 0.51 control — [arXiv:2607.20379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20379) (paper).
- Audit 2 ("Understanding Confabulation and Rethinking Reconstruction", Lek, Xia, Chen, Chen, 2026-09-27): released NLAs have assertion risk 76.3–92.6% and confabulated-token coverage 55.4–74.8% (Qwen2.5-7B worst at 92.6/74.8); confabulation index correlates with training FVE (r = 0.78 Qwen, 0.96 Gemma, 0.90 Apertus); Flow-NLA (diffusion-likelihood reward) keeps utility while curbing confabulation — [arXiv:2609.33702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33702) (paper).
- Bauer et al. found NLA-style embedding-replacement injection performed "significantly worse" than AO additive injection on Qwen3-8B — [arXiv:2606.02609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02609) (paper). CHIVE found NLAs give no uplift for counterfactual behaviour prediction (see Q7) — [arXiv:2608.16747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16747) (paper).

### Inferences
- **Reader, not self:** the AV is a separately RL-trained copy reading a frozen target; "train Claude to explain its own activations" (blog wording) refers to weight initialization, not to the deployed subject reporting on itself (inference from the architecture above).
- **Redundant in NeuralScope:** a free-text activation describer for Qwen2.5-1.5B L18 already exists (locally runnable 1.5B AV/AR, or via Neuronpedia API); building another would not be a contribution (inference).
- **Missing:** per-claim calibration/abstention (none found), claim-level causal verification (only anecdotal steering), and an external-observer baseline for "is this information privileged?" (CHIVE suggests often not) (inference).

### Gaps
- Whether the dormantx Qwen2.5-1.5B AV is SFT-only or RL-trained is unclear (sidecar says `stage: sl`); its FVE is unpublished in what I read. Neuronpedia's sources API lists the AR field as the AV repo name, while the model card links `andyx10/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-NLA-L18-ar` — likely a metadata duplication.

## Q4. The Assistant Axis

### Takeaway
"The Assistant Axis" (Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish, Lindsey; arXiv:2601.10387, 2026-01-15; MATS/Anthropic Fellows/Oxford/Anthropic) finds that the leading direction of a "persona space" built from 275 role vectors measures how Assistant-like the model is; steering and "activation capping" along it causally change persona adoption and cut persona-jailbreak harm ~60% without capability loss. It is exposed as a Neuronpedia demo (capped Llama 3.3 70B), a `PROJECTION_CAP` steering method/preset, HF vectors (Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B, Llama 3.3 70B) and a GitHub pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- Method: 275 roles × 5 system prompts × 240 extraction questions (1,200 rollouts/role); judge gpt-4.1-mini labels role-play fully/somewhat/none; role vector = mean post-MLP residual over response tokens at the middle layer; PCA over 377–463 role vectors; Assistant Axis = mean(default-Assistant activation) − mean(fully-role-playing vectors), computed per layer; cos(axis, PC1) >0.60 at all layers, >0.71 at the middle layer — [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (paper).
- Models: Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B (thinking disabled), Llama 3.3 70B; PC1 role loadings correlate >0.92 across models; axis also present in base models — [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (paper).
- Causal tests: steering away raises adoption of human/non-human/"mystical" personas; persona jailbreaks succeed 65.3–88.5% (vs 0.5–4.5% baseline), and steering toward the Assistant reduces harmful responses; first-turn axis projection correlates with second-turn harm rate (r = 0.39–0.52) — [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (paper).
- Activation capping h ← h − v·min(⟨h,v⟩ − τ, 0), τ = 25th percentile of projections, layers 46–53/64 (Qwen) and 56–71/80 (Llama): "decrease the rate of harmful responses by nearly 60% without impacting performance" on IFEval, MMLU Pro, GSM8k, EQ-Bench — [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (paper).
- Drift triggers: "conversations demanding meta-reflection on the model's processes or featuring emotionally vulnerable users" — [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (paper).
- Exposure: [neuronpedia.org/assistant-axis](https://www.neuronpedia.org/assistant-axis) (capped Llama 3.3 70B chat; vector dashboard `llama3.3-70b-it/40-neuronpedia-resid-post/101874252`) — [assistant-axis post](https://github.com/hijohnnylin/neuronpedia/blob/main/apps/webapp/app/blog/posts/assistant-axis.mdx) (code-verified); steer preset alias `assistant-cap` uses `PROJECTION_CAP` on `llama3.3-70b-it` layer 55 — [presets route](https://github.com/hijohnnylin/neuronpedia/blob/main/apps/webapp/app/api/steer/presets/route.tsx) (code-verified); vectors on [HF lu-christina/assistant-axis-vectors](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lu-christina/assistant-axis-vectors) (Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B, Llama 3.3 70B; 1.24 GB) (dataset card text via search excerpt; also stated in the repo README, code-verified); pipeline in [safety-research/assistant-axis](https://github.com/safety-research/assistant-axis), which has no LICENSE file (code-verified).
- Limitations: fuzzy LLM-judged behaviours, three non-frontier dense models, synthetic users, linearity assumption — [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (paper).

### Inferences
- **Redundant in NeuralScope:** any custom persona-drift monitor/capper — the recipe, code and a hosted steering method exist; only the Qwen2.5-1.5B axis itself would need computing (inference).
- **Relevance/missing for self-knowledge:** the axis is an external monitor; nothing tests whether the model knows where it sits on the axis. Because meta-reflection prompts are a documented drift trigger, SelfLab's self-report elicitation may itself shift the model off-Assistant — a confound worth logging (project the axis during self-report prompts) (inference).

### Gaps
- No Qwen2.5-scale (1–2B) replication found; the API exposure of arbitrary user-supplied axis vectors (vs presets) was not verified.

## Q5. Activation Oracles (AOs): architecture, recipe, releases, OOD results, failure modes, and calibration work

### Takeaway
AOs (Karvonen et al., arXiv:2512.15674, 2025-12-17, v2 2026-01-06; ICML 2026 oral) are LoRA-fine-tuned copies of the target that receive target residual activations additively injected (norm-matched) after layer 1 at " ?" placeholder positions and answer arbitrary questions. Trained on ~1M examples (SPQA/LatentQA + 7 classification tasks + self-supervised past/future-token prediction) they recover fine-tuned secrets never seen in training. Follow-ups document vagueness (~49% generic), hallucination, text-inversion confounds, concept-specific "anti-reading", and uselessness of verbalized confidence. **Calibration work exists** (Torrielli, Schneider-Kamp, Galke Poech, arXiv:2605.26045): verbalized confidence is uninformative, but sampling agreement and candidate scoring are calibrated.

### Cited Findings
**Architecture and recipe**
- Oracle prompt "Layer ℓ: <ACT>…<ACT> question"; placeholder " ?"; "After the second transformer layer… adding a norm-matched steering vector": h′ᵢ = hᵢ + ‖hᵢ‖·vᵢ/‖vᵢ‖; addition chosen because LatentQA-style replacement caused 20× (layer 0) / 100,000× (layer 1) norm growth; layer 1 beat layer 0 by 1–11% on Llama-3.3-70B — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper). Code: `SPECIAL_TOKEN = " ?"`, prefix `"Layer: {ℓ}\n" + " ?"*K`, `hook_onto_layer = 1`, HF hook does `resid[pos] = normalize(v)·‖resid[pos]‖·coef + resid[pos]`, `layer_percents = [25, 50, 75]`, target activations collected `with model.disable_adapter()` (i.e., the frozen base is the target, the LoRA-adapted copy is the reader) — [steering_hooks.py](https://github.com/adamkarvonen/activation_oracles/blob/main/nl_probes/utils/steering_hooks.py), [sft_config.py](https://github.com/adamkarvonen/activation_oracles/blob/main/nl_probes/configs/sft_config.py), [dataset_utils.py](https://github.com/adamkarvonen/activation_oracles/blob/main/nl_probes/utils/dataset_utils.py) (code-verified).
- Read layers 25/50/75% depth during training (50% at eval); ~1M examples (~65M tokens); LoRA r=64, α=128, dropout 0.05, all linear, lr 1e-5, batch 16; 10 H100-h (Qwen3-8B), 12 H100-h (Gemma-2-9B), 90 H200-h (Llama-3.3-70B); Claude Haiku 3.5 AO uses layer-0 replacement and full fine-tuning — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper).
- Oracle = same base as target: "each Activation Oracle is trained on activations from its base instruct model and applied to audit either that same model or fine-tuned variants of it" — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper).

**Releases**
- MIT code; README: "pre-trained oracle weights for… 12 different models across the Gemma-2, Gemma-3, Qwen3, and Llama 3 families" plus a later Qwen3.6-27B oracle (HF repo created 2026-09-25; needs `main` branch); an HF search returns 21 adapter repos (incl. learning-rate-sweep variants) covering Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B/14B/32B, Gemma-2-9B/27B-IT, Gemma-3-1B/4B/12B/27B-IT, Llama-3.2-1B/3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3.6-27B; README warns "the smaller models (1-4B) tend to have worse OOD eval performance"; **no Qwen2.5 oracle** — [README/LICENSE](https://github.com/adamkarvonen/activation_oracles), [HF collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/adamkarvonen/activation-oracles) (code-verified).

**OOD results**
- Secret keeping (models from Cywiński et al.): Taboo (Gemma-2-9B-IT) AO 0.205 vs SPQA-only 0.044, best white-box 0.041, best black-box 0.072; User Gender 0.970 vs 0.820/0.870/0.977; Secret Side Constraint (Llama-3.3-70B) 0.590 vs 0.493/0.522/0.968; "match or exceed white-box baselines on all four tasks and the best overall baseline on 3 of 4" — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper). Model organisms: [Eliciting Secret Knowledge, arXiv:2510.01070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01070) (paper, abstract only).
- Emergent-misalignment model diffing on activation *differences*: AO 2.00/5 vs ADL 2.03 vs black-box 1.50 — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper).
- PersonaQA (facts fine-tuned into the target): open-ended accuracy rises from ~5% to ~14% with the full mixture (Claude AO 35%); the fine-tuned models themselves drop to ~55% on yes/no versions — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper).
- Data diversity and quantity both help (Qwen3-8B Taboo 0.302→0.459→0.495) — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper).
- Authors' limitations: "because it is not trained to express uncertainty, it will frequently produce an answer even when confidence is low"; "black-box interpretability"; narrow benchmarks; prompting matched AOs on an anecdotal hallucination-detection test — [arXiv:2512.15674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15674) (paper).

**Failure modes (2026 follow-ups)**
- "Current activation oracles are hard to use" (Jakkli, Rajamanoharan, Nanda; LessWrong, 2026-03): Qwen3-8B/32B AOs; 49.4% of responses generic across 77 probes; sycophancy AUC 0.55 vs 0.77 for dense probes; text inversion; no uncertainty admission — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LXQBcztrWKhtcgQfJ/current-activation-oracles-are-hard-to-use) (paper/blog).
- "Building Better Activation Oracles" (Bauer, De Schamphelaere, Karvonen, Luick, Nanda; arXiv:2606.02609, 2026-06): defines the text-inversion confound; new CoT-based conversational dataset (+0.244→+0.310 AObench), best single layer 62% depth, 5 contiguous layers, on-policy lens data, 2× injection strength; full recipe +0.244→+0.435; AUC 0.83 vs accuracy 0.50 on sycophancy (Qwen AOs default to "No"); consensus@10 (≥0.8 agreement) gives 94.3% precision at 19.4% coverage on Taboo; "we were able to match linear probe performance, but never significantly exceed it"; a no-activation, text-only reader trained on the same objective performed "similar" (weak, unreported evidence); AObench includes an activation-swap test ("same set of tokens but sourced from different upstream contexts") — [arXiv:2606.02609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02609), [code](https://github.com/japhba/activation_oracles) (paper).
- "When Activation Oracles Learn Not to Read" (Bersia, Gaintseva; arXiv:2607.23379, 2026-07-25): AOs fine-tuned on a Taboo subject become concept-specific anti-readers — exact recovery 94% (base AO) / 71% (cross FT-AO) / 35% (own FT-AO); the concept stays linearly decodable inside the AO; suppression arises in the readout pathway — [arXiv:2607.23379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23379) (paper).
- Instructed suppression by the subject evades AOs (oracle returns baseline confidence) — [arXiv:2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664) (paper).

**Calibration of AOs (the only dedicated study found)**
- Torrielli, Schneider-Kamp, Galke Poech, "Confidence and Calibration of Activation Oracles…", arXiv:2605.26045 (submitted 2026-05-25, v2 2026-08-03); 4 oracles (Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B from Karvonen; community Gemma-3-27B; new Qwen3.6-27B), 20-word Taboo task, 6,000 samples/method/oracle; inject at oracle layer 1, read target at 50% (Qwen3-8B L18/36), 75%, 25%, 75% — [arXiv:2605.26045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26045), [code](https://github.com/federicotorrielli/probabilistic_activation_oracles) (paper).
- Results: direct numeric self-report AUROC 0.404–0.531, ECE 0.56–0.78, anti-calibrated on Qwen3.6-27B; forced choice over the 20 candidates doubles accuracy (e.g., 0.414→0.646 Qwen3-8B) with AUROC 0.922–0.959; k=20 sampling agreement at tuned temperature is the only free-text method with ECE 0.04–0.12 on all oracles; constrained label scoring AUROC 0.68–0.82; post-hoc calibrators reach ECE 0.01–0.03 with labels — [arXiv:2605.26045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26045) (paper).

### Inferences
- **What AOs demonstrate:** a *trained reader* (LoRA copy) can extract information from frozen activations that is absent from the input text (fine-tuned secrets, activation differences). They do **not** demonstrate that the subject model has privileged access to its own states: the subject never answers; the reader is trained; and a text-only reader reportedly performs similarly on Bauer's tasks (inference from the cited facts).
- **Redundant in NeuralScope:** "inject activations into the model and ask it questions" (the requester already knows) — plus consensus filtering and basic confidence readouts for AOs (inference).
- **Missing:** calibration has been studied only for one closed-vocabulary task (secret word) and for *reader* confidence, not for claims about the model's own computation; no AO study compares against an external model given identical activations and training (Li et al. in Q7 is closest); no causal verification of AO claims (inference).

### Gaps
- No AO calibration work beyond Taboo was found (searches: "activation oracles calibration", "activation oracle uncertainty abstention", alphaXiv discovery on AO successors). No Qwen2.5 AO exists.

## Q6. Precursors: LatentQA (Pan et al.), SelfIE (Chen et al.), Patchscopes (Ghandeharioun et al.)

### Takeaway
Patchscopes and SelfIE are training-free "same weights, separate forward pass" readers (patch/replace a hidden state into an interpretation prompt); LatentQA trains a LoRA copy (read layer 15 → write layer 0) on 16.7k synthetic QA pairs. All three were later shown to be largely matchable by text-only baselines on their standard feature-extraction benchmarks and to leak the verbalizer's own knowledge.

### Cited Findings
**LatentQA** — Alexander Pan, Lijie Chen, Jacob Steinhardt (UC Berkeley), arXiv:2412.08686 (2024-12-11; v2 2026-03-23), ICLR 2026 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08686) (paper).
- Decoder "always initialized as a copy of the target LLM"; read layer k=15, write layer ℓ=0 (sweep Table 6); activations substituted for dummy "???" tokens; LoRA r=32, α=64, lr 1e-4, batch 128 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08686) (paper); config `min_layer_to_read: 15`, `layer_to_write: 0` — [train_config.py](https://github.com/aypan17/latentqa/blob/main/lit/configs/train_config.py) (code-verified). Code Apache-2.0; released decoder `aypan17/latentqa_llama-3-8b-instruct` — [README](https://github.com/aypan17/latentqa) (code-verified).
- Data: 16,732 points (4,670 goals, 3,359 personas, 8,703 extractive QA) generated with o1-preview; "activation masking" to stop reading control tokens — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08686) (paper).
- Results: hidden system prompts +10.8% absolute over GPT-4 given the full dialog; relational extraction +38.2% over prior LatentQA systems and +32.2% over linear probing; hidden-persona accuracy 30/12 (1B), 96/88 (3B), 100/92 (8B) easy/hard; Gemma-3-4b-it 90/80 vs SelfIE 24/16 vs prompting 76/76; control via decoder gradients reduces CrowS-Pairs bias (3.70 vs 4.05) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08686) (paper).

**SelfIE** — Haozhe Chen, Carl Vondrick, Chengzhi Mao (Columbia/Mila/McGill), arXiv:2403.10949 (2024-03), ICML 2024 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10949) (paper); venue per [AVPO references](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34033) (secondary).
- Replace placeholder [X] hidden state at layer k of an interpretation forward pass of the *same* LLM with h from layer ℓ*; k=3, [X] repeated 5×, prompt "[INST] [X] [/INST] Sure, I'll summarize your message:"; no training; relevancy score = P(token | inserted) − P(token | not inserted) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10949) (paper). Code default `k=1` in `interpret()`, `k=2` in `interpret_vectors()`, raw hidden state inserted; repo has no LICENSE file — [interpret.py](https://github.com/tonychenxyz/selfie/blob/main/selfie/interpret.py) (code-verified).
- Results (LLaMA-2-70B-Chat): TextWorld entity states ≈ 100-shot linear probe (60–80% of information); reinforcement control cut prompt-injection harmful success from 89.06% to 4.4% while keeping 95.85% of facts; k<20 best; 7B/13B fail the instruction format 27.17%/17.68% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10949) (paper).

**Patchscopes** — Asma Ghandeharioun, Avi Caciularu, Adam Pearce, Lucas Dixon, Mor Geva (Google Research/Tel Aviv U.), arXiv:2401.06102, ICML 2024 (PMLR 235) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06102) (paper); code [PAIR-code/interpretability](https://github.com/PAIR-code/interpretability) (Apache-2.0, code-verified).
- Formal: source (S, i, M, ℓ) → target (T, i*, f, M*, ℓ*); few-shot identity prompt "cat->cat; 135->135; hello->hello; ?"; logit/tuned lens are special cases (ℓ*=L); cross-model M→M* with an affine f — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06102) (paper).
- Results: token-identity Patchscope beats logit/tuned lens from layer 10 upward, "up to 98%" gain at layers 18–22; zero-shot feature extraction beats probes on 6/12 tasks; Vicuna 7B→13B cross-model patching improves entity description; multi-hop correction 50% vs CoT 35.71% vs vanilla 19.57% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06102) (paper).
- Own caveat: post-patching computation "can augment the representation with additional information, hence, this approach does not guarantee that the patched representation itself stores all that information" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06102) (paper).

**Critique covering all three** — Millicent Li, Alberto Ceballos Arroyo, Giordano Rogers, Naomi Saphra, Byron Wallace, "Do Activation Verbalization Methods Convey Privileged Information?", arXiv:2509.13316, ICML 2026 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13316), [code](https://github.com/millicentli/verb_faithfulness) (paper):
- A zero-shot model given only input text matches LIT (Llama3 avg 0.64 vs 0.64) and beats Patchscopes (0.38) on the feature-extraction benchmark; inversion-then-answer recovers comparable performance; cross-family LIT (Llama→Ministral) collapses (0.00–0.20); on PersonaQA-Fantasy the verbalizer answers with knowledge the target lacks (0.12–0.50 accuracy where 0 is faithful) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13316) (paper).

### Inferences
- Patchscopes/SelfIE are the only members that use *unmodified* subject weights, but they read an activation in a *new, out-of-context* forward pass; this tests "can the model's downstream circuitry decode this vector", not "does the model know its current state" (inference).
- **Redundant in NeuralScope:** Patchscope/SelfIE-style decoding is a few lines on top of existing activation injection; a logit-lens view is a degenerate Patchscope (inference).
- **Missing:** text-only/inversion baselines were absent in the originals; no calibration; causal checks only on the reader side (SelfIE relevancy) or via control, never per decoded claim (inference).

### Gaps
- Meta-Models (Costarelli, Allen, Field; [arXiv:2410.02472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02472)) — a separate model (phi-2) reads Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct activations swapped in "immediately after the embedding layer" and generalizes to lie detection (paper, abstract/highlights only). InversionView (Huang, Panwar, Goyal, Hahn; NeurIPS 2024; [arXiv:2405.17653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17653)) samples inputs from an activation's ε-preimage via a trained conditional decoder and causally verifies circuits (paper) — both are external-reader methods; not studied further.

## Q7. "Training Language Models to Explain Their Own Computations" and 2025–2026 successors

### Takeaway
Li, Guo, Huang, Steinhardt, Andreas (arXiv:2511.08579; Transluce/MIT) train explainer LMs on ground truth from auto-interp, activation patching and input ablation, and find target models are better explained by (fine-tuned copies of) themselves than by other models — but the advantage tracks activation-space similarity and the authors note the fixed-target/trained-explainer setup "does not constitute self-explanation in the strictest sense". Successors split: Introspective Coupling (Guo et al., 2026) finds explanations track the model's *own drifting* behaviour; CHIVE (Karvonen et al., 2026) finds activation tools give no uplift for counterfactual prediction and **no privileged access** for self-trained vs cross-trained counterfactual predictors.

### Cited Findings
**Li et al. 2025 (arXiv:2511.08579; v1 2025-11-11, v3 2026-02-09)** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08579), [code (MIT)](https://github.com/TransluceAI/introspective-interp), [HF collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/Transluce/training-language-models-to-explain-their-own-computations) (paper; code-verified licence/READMEs).
- Hypothesis: "Models trained to explain their own internal computations can do so more accurately than other models trained to explain them"; footnote: "This paper treats the target model as fixed and only fine-tunes the explainer model… does not constitute self-explanation in the strictest sense" — (paper).
- Injection: v inserted "as a continuous token… at the embedding layer of the explainer"; learned per-layer projection Π_ℓ when widths differ — (paper); code replaces the special `continuous_rep` token embedding (projected via `embed_projs` if dims differ); no rescaling seen in that function — [continuous_base.py](https://github.com/TransluceAI/introspective-interp/blob/main/model/continuous_base.py) (code-verified).
- Task 1, feature descriptions (target Llama-3.1-8B, LlamaScope 131K residual SAE features, Neuronpedia labels, LM simulator): LM-judge / simulator on held-out SAE features — Llama-3.1-8B self 76.2/45.1 (LoRA 76.0/45.6), Llama-3-8B 77.0/44.6, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 77.1/42.7, Qwen3-8B 70.3/40.6, Llama-3.1-70B random-projection 63.9/39.5 vs pre-trained projection 74.1/45.2, nearest-neighbour 58.5/33.7, SelfIE best-of-5 40.1/36.4, gold SAE labels 100/43.3; OOD full activations: self 49.7 vs Qwen3-8B 21.1 — (paper, Table 1).
- Similarity confound: dot-product similarity with Llama-3.1-8B activations is 96.61% (Llama-3-8B), 85.27% (3.1-Instruct), 21.48% (70B with projection), 0.10% (Qwen3-8B), and "a model's ability to explain (its own or others') features is correlated with the degree of similarity between the explainer and target models" — (paper, Table 4, §3.4).
- Task 2, activation patching (CounterFact; block-averaged patches over four layer blocks; predict has-changed and new output): Qwen3-8B target — self 64.0 EM / 80.2 F1 / 71.0 content vs Llama explainer 54.1/73.9/65.4 vs untrained 5.0/24.4/18.8; Llama-3.1-8B target — self 48.6/77.5/54.6 vs Qwen explainer 41.7/75.1/47.6; ablating the activation input costs ~3–4 points; layer/token are recoverable from the activation (98.7% exact match) — (paper, Table 5).
- Task 3, input ablation (MMLU with hints): "some models appear universally stronger explainers (e.g. Qwen outperforms Llama across the board); however, we still observe evidence of privileged access" — [Transluce post](https://transluce.org/self-explanations) (paper).
- Data efficiency: self-explaining "achieving comparable results with only 0.8% of the training data" of nearest-neighbour — (paper).

**Introspective Coupling (Guo, Ruis, Andreas, Li; arXiv:2606.32038, 2026-06-30)** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32038) (paper):
- Train Qwen3-8B (also Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B) on fixed counterfactual explanations ("if the cue were removed, would your answer change?") from its *initial* checkpoint with a KL regularizer on behaviour; tasks Hint-MMLU, AITA, refusal. Explanations match the model's *current* behaviour better than their training labels ("Self > Orig"); holds even with Llama-derived labels (Self EM 88% at α=0 → 69% at α=1); patching effects on behaviour and explanation logits correlate r=0.89 (0.527 without regularization); explanations track behaviour shifts from auxiliary post-training (WildChat, warm-assistant data, refusal data) without explanation labels.

**CHIVE (Karvonen, Ong, Kantamneni, Marks; arXiv:2608.16747, 2026-08-17)** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16747), [code](https://github.com/adamkarvonen/chive) (paper):
- Agentic pipeline finds in-the-wild behaviours (WildChat) and runs 5–15 counterfactual prompt edits each; predictor agents (Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, Gemini-3.1-Pro) given AO/NLA/SAE tools get **no uplift** over transcript-only (Gemma-3-27B-IT AUROC: baseline 0.814, AO 0.810, NLA 0.808, SAE 0.812; Gemini is hurt by NLA 0.654); NLA outputs state the causal relation in 0.6% of positive claims; tools restricted to read-only by design.
- Training target models to predict their own counterfactual behaviour generalizes to held-out and OOD sources; but "We find no evidence of privileged access… the cross-trained model matches the self-trained model" (Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct).

**Other 2025–2026 activation verbalizers / latent reporters**
- Predictive Concept Decoders (Huang, Choi, Johnson, Schwettmann, Steinhardt; Transluce; arXiv:2512.15712, 2025-12-17): encoder (linear + TopK, 32,768 concepts, k=16) reads Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct layer 15; decoder = subject weights + LoRA, written at layer 0; pretrain on FineWeb next-token (18–144M tokens), finetune on SynthSys; matches LatentQA at ≥36M tokens; reveals secret-hint use "that prompting cannot elicit" and jailbreak awareness the subject "cannot verbalize" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15712), [transluce.org/pcd](https://transluce.org/pcd) (paper).
- Universal Activation Verbalizer (Zhao, He, Wang, Payani, Li, Du; arXiv:2605.25903, 2026-05-25, v2 2026-08-31; EMNLP 2026 per a later citation): shared decoder + donor adapters; "self- and cross-decoding are largely comparable", self-decoding better mainly on fine-grained fact retrieval; beats AO/LatentQA on Qwen3-4B; SelfIE/Patchscopes near-zero on facts — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25903) (paper).
- AVPO (Zhao et al.; arXiv:2609.34033, 2026-09-27): question-agnostic inverter (DPO) + separate frozen QA model; a cross-model Qwen3-4B inverter scores 0.624 vs AO 0.423, LatentQA 0.475, UAV 0.492 on Llama-3.1-8B activations; fabricates patient names 0.10 vs 0.87–0.99 for QA-trained verbalizers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34033) (paper).
- Activation Controllability Benchmark (Kowalski, Fonseca Rivera, Macar, Africa; arXiv:2608.21664, 2026-08-21): 25 open models can raise/suppress/dial concept projections on instruction (S = 0.059–0.538; Llama 3.1 8B highest; Qwen 2.5 72B 0.339); **no model can target a specific layer**; instructed suppression evades probes (5–21% TPR@1%FPR), J-lens, AOs and NLAs (0/500 NLA mentions for Gemma 3 27B) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664) (paper).
- Binder et al., "Looking Inward" (ICLR 2025; arXiv:2410.13787): self-prediction-trained M1 beats cross-trained M2 at predicting M1's hypothetical behaviour (Llama-70B 48.5% vs GPT-4o 31.8%); self-trained models are better calibrated (MAD 23.0→8.7 vs 21.3 cross); fails on complex/OOD tasks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787) (paper, abstract/highlights).
- Seen but not read (titles/abstract only): SAEVerbalizer [arXiv:2608.13538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13538); One Adapter Pair per Model [arXiv:2608.09521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09521); Selecting the Most Informative Tokens in NLAs [arXiv:2609.37040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37040); Cycle-consistent AOs ([LessWrong, Chalnev 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Nf2sKaNNdxE2ssxbp/cycle-consistent-activation-oracles-1), secondary); Lindsey, "Emergent introspective awareness" ([Transformer Circuits 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html); arXiv:2601.01828 per [2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664), secondary); Ji-An et al., metacognitive monitoring/control of internal activations (arXiv:2505.13763, secondary via [2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664)).

### Inferences
- **Pre-emption risk for SelfLab (inference):** "counterfactual self-prediction" is already studied in three forms — activation-patching outcome prediction with self-vs-other explainers (Li et al.), prompt-edit counterfactual prediction with self-vs-cross training (CHIVE; null for privileged access), and cue-ablation explanations that track own behaviour (Guo et al.). SelfLab's novelty must therefore lie in (a) predictions about interventions on the model's *own internals* made by the unmodified or minimally-trained subject itself, (b) matched external-observer baselines with equal information, (c) calibration/abstention, and (d) execution of the predicted intervention for causal scoring.
- **Evidence status:** "privileged access" in Li et al. is plausibly explained by representational similarity (Llama-3-8B explains Llama-3.1-8B as well as itself; pre-aligning 70B recovers most of the gap), and CHIVE finds none for counterfactual prediction; Binder and Guo et al. show self-advantages at the *behavioural* level (inference).
- **Self-localisation:** the activation-control finding that models cannot target a layer is direct negative evidence on layer-level self-access/control; Li et al.'s 98.7% layer/token recovery is a *reader* localizing provenance, not self-localisation (inference).

### Gaps
- I did not read Li et al.'s main-text Table 2 (input ablation numbers) or Section 7 discussion in full; CHIVE's Appendix D.4 numbers for self vs cross were not read (only the main-text statement).

## Q8. Subject self-access vs separate-reader access: what each method shows, and designs that separate them

### Takeaway
Almost every activation-to-language method is evidence of **reader access**: a fine-tuned copy (LatentQA, AO, NLA, PCD, Li et al.) or another model (UAV, AVPO, Meta-Models, cross-model Patchscopes) decodes frozen activations; cross-model readers are competitive, text-only baselines often match, and readers import their own knowledge. Only J-lens swap→report experiments (Claude), Lindsey-style injection-detection, and behavioural self-prediction work (Binder; Guo et al.) probe the subject's *own* reports, and none combines mechanism-level targets, an equal-information external observer, calibration and executed interventions.

### Cited Findings
- AO targets are collected with the adapter disabled, i.e., reader ≠ subject weights — [dataset_utils.py](https://github.com/adamkarvonen/activation_oracles/blob/main/nl_probes/utils/dataset_utils.py) (code-verified).
- NLA AV/AR are trained copies of a frozen target — [NLA paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/index.html) (paper).
- Li et al.: explainer fine-tuned, target fixed; "self" advantage correlates with activation similarity — [arXiv:2511.08579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08579) (paper).
- Cross-model decoding is comparable to self-decoding (UAV) or better (AVPO) — [arXiv:2605.25903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25903), [arXiv:2609.34033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34033) (paper).
- Text-only / inversion baselines match LIT and Patchscopes on feature extraction; verbalizers use their own parametric knowledge — [arXiv:2509.13316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13316) (paper); a text-only reader was "similar" to AOs in Bauer et al.'s sprint — [arXiv:2606.02609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02609) (paper).
- PCD surfaces hint use and jailbreak awareness that the subject fails to self-report (reader > subject) — [arXiv:2512.15712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15712) (paper).
- CHIVE: no privileged access (self-trained ≈ cross-trained) for counterfactual prediction — [arXiv:2608.16747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16747) (paper).
- Subject-level evidence: J-lens swaps change the model's own verbal report (88% top-5) — [J-lens paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html); Binder cross-prediction design (self M1 > cross M2 trained on M1's behaviour) — [arXiv:2410.13787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787); Guo et al. Self > Orig under behaviour drift — [arXiv:2606.32038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32038) (paper).
- Validity threats: readers can learn concept-specific blind spots — [arXiv:2607.23379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23379); subjects can suppress concepts to evade readers — [arXiv:2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664); reconstruction scores can be gamed — [arXiv:2607.20379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20379) (paper).

### Inferences (proposed discriminating design — all inference)
1. **Four matched arms per query**, same targets and scoring: (A) *Subject-self*: the unmodified subject asked in-context about its own computation (no injection); (A′) *Subject-self trained*: subject fine-tuned on self-labels with a behavioural KL regularizer (Guo et al.) so object-level behaviour is held fixed; (B) *Same-weights reader*: the subject's weights given injected activations in a fresh pass (Patchscope/SelfIE/AO-style); (C) *Other-model reader*: a different model with a learned map, trained on the identical label set and budget (Li/Binder M2); (D) *Text-only observer*: a strong model given the full transcript but no activations, trained on the same labels. Privileged self-access = A or A′ beats max(B, C, D) on held-out items under proper scoring (log-loss/Brier, ECE, AUROC, selective-prediction curves).
2. **Targets not recoverable from text:** predictions about the subject's own internal interventions (head ablations, activation patches, steering) whose outcomes are then *executed* and compared — NeuralScope's existing patching/ablation sweeps supply ground truth for free.
3. **Similarity control:** include a "twin" reader (e.g., a sibling checkpoint differing only by a controlled fine-tune) and mirror-image pairs (A explains B, B explains A) to separate self-identity from representational similarity (the Li et al. confound).
4. **Anti-text-inversion controls:** activation-swap (same tokens, different upstream context), shuffled/mismatched activations, inversion-then-answer baseline.
5. **Drift test:** after training reporters, alter the subject's internals (fine-tune/steer/ablate); privileged access predicts self-reports track the new internals while frozen external readers do not (Binder behaviour-change test; Guo et al.).
6. **Adversarial check:** measure activation controllability of the subject (2608.21664 protocol) because a model able to modulate its activations could also shape what readers see.

### Gaps
- I found no study that runs arms A–D together on mechanism-level targets with calibration and executed interventions (searches: alphaXiv discovery on activation verbalizers/self-interpretation/introspection 2025–2026; the successors listed above). This is the most defensible open niche for SelfLab, subject to other researchers' coverage of the introspection literature.
